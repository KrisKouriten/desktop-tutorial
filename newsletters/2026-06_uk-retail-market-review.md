## Executive Summary

May delivered a mixed but cautiously improved picture for UK retail, though the underlying fragility remains acute. CPI eased to 2.8% in April (from 3.3% in March) as the energy price cap reduction fed through, providing momentary relief. GfK consumer confidence ticked up 2 points to -23, defying the -28 forecast, though GfK's Neil Bellamy warned the uptick is "unlikely to mark a sustained recovery" as the savings index crashed 10 points -- households are dipping into reserves to meet everyday expenses.

The ONS confirmed the post-Easter reality: April retail sales volumes fell 1.3% MoM, the largest monthly drop since May 2025. Fuel sales collapsed 10.2% (the worst since November 2020) as consumers delayed filling up amid rising prices. Clothing fell 2.4% as variable weather and price sensitivity hit demand. The BRC-KPMG monitor showed total April sales down 3.0% YoY, though the Easter timing distortion means March-April combined rose 1.5%.

The CBI survey improved from its historic -68 low to -46 in May, but remains deeply negative -- the least negative reading in three months is hardly cause for celebration. Retailers expect to pare back both investment and hiring plans.

The labour market deteriorated further: unemployment eased slightly to 5.0% but payrolled employees fell 210,000 YoY. Vacancies dropped to 705,000, the lowest since 2021. Youth unemployment hit 16.2% -- the highest in over a decade.

The Bank of England held rates at 3.75% through April and May. Governor Bailey said on 29 May the Bank is "in no rush to raise rates" while the Iran war outcome and growth remain uncertain. The next MPC decision is **18 June 2026**.

Looking ahead, the confirmed **July energy price cap rise to GBP 1,862 (+13%, +GBP 221/year)** looms as the next major consumer shock. Wimbledon (29 June), the King's Speech legislative agenda, and half-term trading provide the key events backdrop.

## The May Retail Market at a Glance

*Sources: Retail Gazette, ONS, BRC, CBI, GfK, Barclays*

### The Easter Hangover Arrives

April's official data, released in May, confirmed what forward indicators had warned: the March resilience was a timing mirage. ONS retail sales volumes fell 1.3% MoM in April -- the largest monthly fall since May 2025. Fuel sales collapsed 10.2% as motorists delayed filling up amid rising prices, the worst monthly drop since November 2020. Clothing stores fell 2.4%, which retailers attributed to variable weather and increased consumer price sensitivity.

The BRC-KPMG Retail Sales Monitor painted an even starker picture: total sales fell 3.0% YoY in April. Food sales declined 2.5% (against +8.2% a year ago), and non-food fell 3.3%. Helen Dickinson (BRC CEO) noted the Easter timing shift was the primary driver, but warned that "weaker consumer confidence had also affected spending." When March and April are combined to adjust for Easter, total sales rose a modest 1.5% YoY.

### Record May Heatwave Provides Unexpected Boost

Retail Gazette reported a sharp surge in seasonal demand as the UK experienced a record-breaking May heatwave. Tesco reported ice cube sales tripled week-on-week, sunscreen sales rose almost sixfold, and electric fan sales surged 30x. Barbecues, outdoor furniture, and summer clothing all saw significant uplifts, providing a welcome boost to an otherwise subdued market.

### M&S Results: Resilience Meets Cyber Reality

Marks & Spencer reported FY 2025/26 results on 20 May:
- Statutory PBT fell 24% to GBP 511.8m, primarily due to a GBP 248.5m Ocado Retail impairment
- The retailer warned of a GBP 300m profit hit in the current year from the "highly sophisticated" cyberattack
- Food market share at 4.0% (historic high); food remains the standout performer
- Despite the cyber headwinds, M&S is gaining share, improving underlying profitability, and the balance sheet is strong

### JD Sports: Revenue Growth, Profit Pressure

JD Sports released FY results on 7 May:
- Revenue up 11.7% to GBP 12.7bn
- Warned of US demand risk from Trump tariff policies
- Underlying sales fell 2%, revealing weak discretionary demand
- Share price subsequently surged 25% in the month as the market priced in the worst

### The King's Speech: Legislative Agenda for Retail

Retail Gazette highlighted key implications from the King's Speech (13 May) for the sector:
- EU trade facilitation measures (SPS alignment, border checks reduction)
- Energy transition legislation
- Late payment crackdown
- Financial services reforms
- Enhanced policing measures for retail crime

## 3. PESTEL Analysis: Political

### The King's Speech (13 May 2026)

The government's legislative programme set out measures directly affecting retail:
- **EU trade reset:** Sector-specific single market alignment measures; SPS agreement to reduce food border checks; customs facilitation
- **Energy Bill:** Accelerated transition to clean energy; implications for retail energy costs
- **Late Payments Bill:** Crackdown on extended payment terms -- a lifeline for smaller suppliers
- **Policing and Crime Bill:** Enhanced measures for retail crime (builds on Crime and Policing Bill provisions)
- **Financial Services reforms:** Consumer protection updates

The BRC welcomed the trade and policing measures but warned the legislative burden must not add to the "cost of doing business crisis."

### Local Elections (7 May 2026)

4,851 seats across 134 English councils were contested. Local government reorganisation continues: East Surrey and West Surrey held first elections under new unitary structures. Policy uncertainty persists around planning, business support and regeneration funding in reorganised areas.

### Employment Costs: The Ongoing Squeeze

The April cost wall is now biting:
- Retail employment fell to 2.76 million (record low); 74,000 jobs lost in the past year
- 61% of CFOs plan to reduce staff hours; 45% expect recruitment freezes
- Guaranteed hours reforms remain a major concern: BRC warns 55% of retail roles (part-time) could be affected
- The government launched a GBP 1bn youth jobs scheme in response to the "jobless generation" warning

## 4. PESTEL Analysis: Economic

### Inflation: Temporary Relief

CPI eased to 2.8% in April (from 3.3% in March), primarily due to the April energy price cap reduction lowering household gas and electricity costs. However, this relief is temporary:
- Core CPI remains elevated
- The July energy price cap will rise 13% to GBP 1,862
- Food inflation still forecast >5% through 2026 (BRC)
- Services inflation remains sticky at ~4.3%

## 5. PESTEL Analysis: Social

### Consumer Behaviour: Fragile Recovery

BRC-Opinium data shows spending expectations edged up to +7 in May (from +5 in April), but personal saving expectations held at -8 -- consumers are spending but depleting reserves to do so. The Savings Index crash (-10pts in GfK) confirms this unsustainable pattern.

Key behavioural signals:
- 47% of consumers have not made a big-ticket purchase in 2026
- Shoppers doubling down on loyalty schemes, promotions, own-label and discounters
- Health and beauty (+6.3%) remains structurally resilient
- Travel spending declining (-3.3% in March) as Iran conflict hits holiday confidence

### The May Heatwave Effect

Record May temperatures triggered significant category-specific surges:
- Ice cubes: Sales tripled (Tesco)
- Sunscreen: Up almost 6x (Tesco)
- Electric fans: Up 30x
- Barbecues, outdoor furniture, summer clothing all saw major uplifts

This provides a template for June/July trading if warm weather continues, but also highlights the increasing weather-dependence of UK retail trading.

### GLP-1 Impact Deepening

The GLP-1 medication trend continues to reshape grocery and food-to-go spending. ~4.3 million users reporting 11% reduction in weekly grocery spend and 19% reduction in takeaway. Retailers expanding GLP-1-specific meal ranges (M&S, Morrisons, Asda, Co-op).

### Half-Term Trading (25-29 May)

The Spring Bank Holiday and school half-term week provided the strongest trading opportunity of the month. Family-oriented categories (children's clothing, toys, outdoor, eating out) saw seasonal uplift, though constrained by household budget pressures.

## 7. PESTEL Analysis: Environmental

### July Energy Price Cap: The Next Shock

Ofgem confirmed the July-September 2026 price cap at **GBP 1,862/year** for a typical dual-fuel household, a 13% increase (GBP 221/year) from the current GBP 1,641. Cornwall Insight warns October could be worse. National Energy Action stated: "The potential for another energy crisis is real."

This will directly impact:
- Household disposable income (estimated GBP 18/month additional cost)
- Retailer operating costs (energy bills, cold chain, lighting)
- Consumer confidence (already fragile; energy bills are the #1 concern)

### Packaging EPR: Operational Phase

The packaging EPR scheme is now operational:
- Binary labelling (recycle/do not recycle) mandatory since 1 April
- Fee modulation based on recyclability (RAG rating) from 2026-27
- Plastics base rate: GBP 455/tonne
- Deposit Return Scheme: On track for October 2027

### Weather: May Heatwave Followed by Changeable Conditions

After the record May heatwave, conditions are expected to become more changeable into June. The Met Office outlook for early summer suggests near-normal temperatures with periods of both settled and unsettled weather.
