## Executive Summary

The US-Iran conflict that erupted on 28 February continues to dominate the UK retail landscape. Energy prices remain elevated (Brent crude above $116/barrel, UK wholesale gas up 75%), and the inflationary impact is now clear: CPI jumped to 3.3% in March, driven by transport and motor fuel costs. The Bank of England held rates at 3.75% on 30 April by an 8-1 vote, with Chief Economist Huw Pill the sole dissenter voting for a hike to 4%.

The contradiction between official data and forward-looking surveys is stark. ONS retail sales volumes rose 0.7% MoM in March, and BRC like-for-like sales surged 3.1% YoY -- the strongest since April 2025 -- boosted by an early Easter, Mother's Day and fuel stockpiling. Yet the CBI's April retail survey plunged to -68, the sharpest year-on-year decline in sales in over 40 years (the worst since the series began in 1983), with 77% of firms reporting sales down. The May outlook darkened further to -60, the gloomiest since the Covid pandemic.

Consumer confidence collapsed to -25 (GfK April), the lowest since October 2023, with the sharpest deterioration in economic expectations (-8pts to -51). The "wall of cost" hit retailers on 1 April: business rates revaluation, end of the 40% RHL relief, National Living Wage rising to GBP 12.71/hour, Employment Rights Act provisions, and packaging EPR labelling requirements all went live simultaneously. The BRC estimates employment costs alone have risen by over GBP 5 billion.

The K-shaped recovery deepens: grocery and value retailers (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Lidl) continue to gain share, while discretionary and mid-market segments face acute pressure. The OECD has downgraded UK growth to just 0.5% for 2026. Store closures accelerate: The Original Factory Shop shuttered all stores; Poundland closed ~100 locations; Cancer Research UK closing 88 charity shops by May.

## 2. PESTEL Analysis: Political

### Spring Statement (26 March 2026)

Chancellor Rachel Reeves delivered the Spring Statement with no major new tax changes, reserving fiscal announcements for the Autumn Budget (October/November 2026). The OBR downgraded GDP growth from 1.4% to 1.1% for 2026, with unemployment projected to peak at 5.3%.

Helen Dickinson (BRC CEO): *"The immediate risk is to jobs, especially in retail."*

### Business Rates Overhaul (Effective 1 April 2026)

The single biggest structural cost change for retail this spring. The government replaces the annual RHL relief with a permanent five-multiplier system:

| Multiplier | Rate (p/GBP) | Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| Small business RHL | 38.2p | RHL properties, RV < GBP 51,000 |
| Small business non-RHL | 43.2p | Non-RHL, RV < GBP 51,000 |
| Standard RHL | 43.0p | RHL, RV GBP 51,000-499,999 |
| Standard non-RHL | 48.0p | Non-RHL, RV GBP 51,000-499,999 |
| Large property | 50.8p | All properties, RV GBP 500,000+ |

**Critical:** The 40% temporary RHL relief (capped at GBP 110,000) expires 31 March 2026 and is NOT renewed. Large retailers, supermarkets and distribution centres face higher rates via the 50.8p multiplier. Convenience stores face an average GBP 1,600 increase. A GBP 3.2 billion Transitional Relief scheme caps increases at 5% (small), 15% (medium), 30% (large) in 2026-27.

### Employment Law Changes (6 April 2026)

The Employment Rights Act 2025 introduces the most significant reforms in decades:

- **National Living Wage:** Rises to GBP 12.71/hour (+4.1% for age 21+), GBP 10.85 (18-20), GBP 8.00 (16-17)
- **Statutory Sick Pay:** Payable from day one (3 waiting days abolished); lower earnings limit scrapped; rises to GBP 123.25/week
- **Family leave:** Paternity and unpaid parental leave become day-one rights
- **Fair Work Agency:** New enforcement body launches 7 April
- **Collective redundancy:** Maximum protective award doubles from 90 to 180 days' pay

**Retail impact:** 55% of retail jobs (mostly part-time) affected. Cost of employing a full-time entry-level worker up 10% since April 2025. 52% of retail CFOs plan to reduce hours/overtime; 32% expect to freeze recruitment.

### Tax Changes (April 2026)

- Dividend tax: Basic rate rises to 10.75%; upper rate to 35.75%
- Capital Gains Tax (BADR): Rate increases from 14% to 18%
- Employer NI: Frozen threshold at GBP 5,000 (until 2031) continues to squeeze margins
- New VAT relief for business donations of goods to charity

### UK-EU Trade Policy

- TCA Review underway; three sector-specific agreements possible before year-end
- SPS negotiations ongoing (would reduce food border checks)
- EU CBAM levies began January 2026; UK-EU carbon market linkage under discussion
- Low-value imports reform confirmed (removal of GBP 135 duty relief) but implementation extends to March 2029

### Store Closures

2025 saw 17,349 store closures and 201,953 redundancies (Centre for Retail Research). The cull has accelerated in 2026: The Original Factory Shop shuttered all remaining stores (Week 16); Poundland has now closed ~100 locations; Cancer Research UK closing 88 charity shops by May with up to 100 more planned; the final three GAME stores shutting from April; Lloyds Banking Group confirmed 95 branch closures (May 2026 to March 2027). River Island closed 32 stores in January including Brighton, Oxford and Edinburgh.

## 3. PESTEL Analysis: Economic

### The Iran Conflict -- The Dominant Macro Shock

On 28 February 2026, Israel and the United States began military strikes against Iran. The IEA characterised this as the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market," with ~20 million barrels/day affected via the Strait of Hormuz and production cut by at least 10 million barrels in the Gulf.

| Indicator | Pre-Conflict | Current (Late March) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brent Crude ($/bbl) | ~$73 | >$116 | +59% |
| UK Wholesale Gas | Baseline | +75% in 30 days | +75% |
| Petrol (p/litre) | ~140p | ~154p | +10% |
| Diesel (p/litre) | ~145p | ~174p | +20% |

The physical damage is structural: Ras Laffan (Qatar) is offline for years, Kharg Island has been demolished, South Pars has been struck. Cornwall Insight estimates the energy price cap from July will jump by nearly GBP 200.

### Inflation

**UPDATE (22 April 2026):** CPI rose by 3.3% in the 12 months to March 2026, up from 3.0% in February. The largest upward contribution came from transport, particularly motor fuels. CPIH rose to 3.4%. Core inflation was 3.2%. The Bank of England expects CPI between 3.0-3.5% in Q2/Q3 2026. The OECD predicts UK inflation could hit 4% this year -- up 1.5pp from its previous forecast.

The OBR estimates the energy spike could add 1% to consumer prices by year-end if current conditions persist. Food and non-alcoholic beverages inflation stands at 3.3%, with the BRC forecasting over 5% food price rises through 2026. BoE Governor Andrew Bailey told the BBC on 14 April that the world faces a "very big energy shock" that will push up prices.

### Interest Rates

The MPC voted unanimously to hold Bank Rate at 3.75% on 19 March -- the first unanimous hold since September 2021. All four members who previously voted for cuts shifted to hold. Markets now price in at least two rate hikes in 2026, a sharp reversal from earlier expectations of two cuts.

**UPDATE (30 April 2026):** The MPC voted 8-1 to hold Bank Rate at 3.75%. Chief Economist Huw Pill cast the sole dissenting vote for a 25bp hike to 4%, citing the risk of inflation becoming embedded. The MPC stated that monetary policy cannot influence energy prices but will be set to ensure the economic adjustment occurs in a way that achieves the 2% target sustainably. The next decision is 19 June 2026.

### GDP & Growth

UK GDP was flat in January 2026. The Bank estimates underlying quarterly growth at 0.1-0.2% for Q1.

| Forecaster | 2026 GDP Growth |
|---|---|
| OECD (March 2026) | 0.7% (downgraded from 1.2%) |
| OBR (March 2026) | 1.1% |
| HM Treasury average | 0.9% |
| EY ITEM Club | 0.9% |
| Goldman Sachs | 1.4% |

The OECD's downgrade was the steepest of any major economy. The CNBC headline: *"Iran war will spare no major economy, but the UK is more vulnerable than others."*

### Labour Market

Unemployment at 5.2% (Nov 2025-Jan 2026) -- highest since late 2020 and up from 4.4% a year ago. Youth unemployment (16-24) at 16.0%, up from 14.5%. Vacancies fell to 721,000 (below pre-pandemic levels). Payrolled employees declined 49,000 YoY.

Average real wages grew just 0.7% (including bonuses) and 0.5% (excluding bonuses) -- barely keeping pace with costs. The OBR forecasts unemployment could reach 5.3% in 2026.

### Consumer Confidence (GfK)

**UPDATE (April 2026):** The GfK index plunged to -25 in April (from -21 in March), its lowest since October 2023. Four of five measures fell, with economic expectations deteriorating sharply.

The collapse in confidence is now pervasive: personal finances expectations turned negative for the first time since late 2024, and the general economic outlook measures are at their most pessimistic in over two years. Rising fuel costs are the primary concern, squeezing household budgets and feeding expectations of further price increases.

### Currency

GBP/USD fell from $1.348 to $1.321 in March (-2%), hitting its lowest since December 2025. GBP/EUR traded between 1.152-1.157. Sterling weakness raises import costs for retailers sourcing internationally.

## 4. PESTEL Analysis: Social

### Consumer Behaviour & Sentiment

Consumer spending remains deeply defensive. Q4 2025 household spending grew just 0.1% QoQ. Card spending in February rose only 1.0% YoY -- well below CPIH inflation of 3.2%, meaning real spending declined.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Adults expecting to be worse off in 2026 | 36% |
| Consumers formally budgeting | 51% (up from 46%) |
| Planning to cut eating/drinking out | 62% |
| Planning to cut clothing spend | 52% |
| Planning to cut everyday conveniences | 47% |

The Deloitte Consumer Tracker fell to -11.1% in Q4 2025, the lowest in two years, with both essential and discretionary spending declining YoY. A third of consumers are cutting luxuries/treats; 39% describe themselves as "frugal."

### Easter 2026 Spending (5 April)

Total Easter spending forecast at GBP 2.39 billion (+4% YoY). Over GBP 400 million on Easter eggs alone (80-90 million eggs). However, cocoa inflation of 16.7% has driven egg prices up ~GBP 7 on average, with iconic eggs shrinking by up to 18%.

Easter falls two weeks earlier than 2025, meaning cooler weather and less of the sunshine-driven sales boost that lifted 2025 retail by 5.3% YoY. Spending is polarising: consumers hunt value at discounters while selectively trading up for premium treats, squeezing mid-market.

### Cost of Living: Household Impact

- Real median household incomes fell 1.6% between 2019/20 and 2022/23
- Material deprivation rose from 19.6% to 22.7% of working-age adults
- Food insecurity rose from 7% to 11% of households
- 3.9 million households face mortgage increases averaging GBP 64/month upon remortgaging
- Direct Debit failure rate rose to 2.44%, up 9% YoY -- a sign of financial strain

**April relief measures:** Energy price cap falls 7%; NLW rises 4% (benefiting 2.7 million workers); State Pension rises 4.8% to GBP 241.30/week; two-child limit on Universal Credit removed (lifting an estimated 450,000 children from poverty); rail fare increases capped for the first time in 30 years.

### High Street Footfall

Bank of England agents describe a "lacklustre economy." Footfall growth remains concentrated in diversified locations (food, leisure, mixed-use). Nine in ten Britons still visit bricks-and-mortar destinations during key trading months, but online competition and caution are headwinds.

Fastest-growing high streets (2025 data): Ballycastle (+20%), Carmarthen (+19%), Portrush (+13%) -- only three nationally achieved double-digit growth.

### Sustainability & Ethical Shopping

The "value-action gap" is closing. 73% of consumers recycle; 61% limit single-use plastics; 56% fix/repair rather than replace. Consumers are willing to pay 10% more for environmentally sound products (PwC), but 61% cite cost as the primary barrier. Generic claims like "eco-friendly" are now prohibited unless backed by third-party certification.

The sustainable fashion market reached USD 322.6m in 2025, projected to grow at 22.64% CAGR. Among Gen Z, 25% are buying fewer fashion items overall, with a corresponding rise in second-hand purchasing.

### The GLP-1 Revolution in Retail

Approximately 4.3 million Britons are using GLP-1 medications (Ozempic, Mounjaro), with a potential 7.6 million more considering them. Users report an 11% reduction in weekly grocery spend and 19% reduction in takeaway spend. Steepest declines in ultra-processed categories (snacks, sweets, sugary drinks). Morrisons, M&S, Co-op and Asda have all launched GLP-1-specific ready meal ranges.

### Demographics

UK population projected to reach 70 million in 2026. The "silver economy" is growing: consumers aged 65+ could be responsible for 63p in every pound spent by 2040. High street shops lose an estimated GBP 267 million per month by not being accessible to people with disabilities.

## 5. PESTEL Analysis: Technological

### AI Adoption Accelerating

80% of UK retailers expect online sales growth in 2026 as AI enhances customer experience. UK consumers are Europe's most confident AI adopters (64% trust AI-assisted ecommerce). Almost half of UK adults under 45 already use AI tools for product research and price comparison.

Key developments:
- **Agentic commerce:** 30% of UK adults open to AI acting as a personal shopping agent
- **AI traffic to retail sites:** Surged 693% YoY (Adobe Analytics), converting 31% more often
- **Kingfisher:** AI product recommendations generating GBP 165 million in sales
- **CDS Superstores** (The Range, Wilko, Homebase): Adopted AI-driven forecasting and replenishment

Only 27% of UK retailers say their tech stacks are fully AI-ready. The Retail Technology Show (22-23 April, ExCeL London) -- 450+ exhibitors -- will focus on AI maturity and execution.

### E-commerce

The UK e-commerce market is valued at USD 317.33 billion in 2026. Online sales account for ~30% of all UK retail. January 2026 online sales values rose 14.7% YoY (largest rise since April 2021). Social commerce forecast to generate GBP 12 billion in UK sales by 2026, with livestream events achieving 10-20% conversion rates.

### Self-Checkout: Retreat from Checkout-Free

Over 85% of UK supermarkets have fully integrated self-checkout, with ~80% of supermarket transactions going through SCO. However, fully checkout-free stores have failed commercially: Aldi abandoned its Shop & Go trial in Greenwich (March 2026), following Amazon's earlier retreat from Just Walk Out. Self-checkout-related theft costs retailers an estimated GBP 500 million annually.

### Payment Technology

- Digital wallets projected to account for 21% of all UK transaction volumes by end of 2026
- Contactless payments exceed 60% of UK transactions; cash below one-fifth
- BNPL growing at 11.12% CAGR, now integrated into physical POS
- HM Treasury published the Payments Forward Plan (February 2026)
- Variable Recurring Payments: First live payments expected Q1 2026
- Digital Pound: Design phase throughout 2026; blueprint and decision expected in-year

### Mobile Commerce

Smartphones commanded 54.12% of e-commerce transaction value in 2025. Over 60% of UK consumers prefer mobile shopping. NFC transactions predicted to handle 50% of all UK payments by end of 2026.

### Retail Media

Retail media forecast to overtake combined investment in linear and streaming TV globally by 2026. Tesco and Sainsbury's are building digital ad networks as core revenue platforms.

## 6. PESTEL Analysis: Environmental

### Packaging EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility)

The most significant environmental regulation change for retail in 2026:

- **31 March 2026:** All packaging (except flexible films) must carry binary "recycle" / "do not recycle" labels
- **1 April 2026:** UK Packaging PRO formally commences operations
- **Fee modulation from 2026-27:** Red-amber-green (RAG) recyclability rating; base rate for plastics rises from GBP 423 to GBP 455 per tonne
- **Financial scale:** GBP 1.4 billion invested in Year 1; expected to raise over GBP 1.1 billion annually for local authorities
- **Deposit Return Scheme:** Now set for 1 October 2027

### Climate & Sustainability Reporting

- UK Sustainability Reporting Standards (UK SRS) finalised for voluntary use in early 2026
- Transition Plans required from 19 January 2026 for companies seeking market admission where climate is material
- ESOS Phase 4: Energy-use data to be published publicly for the first time
- Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) continues for companies with turnover GBP 36m+
- New minimum requirements for reporting carbon offsets

### Greenwashing Enforcement

CMA published updated supply chain guidance (22 January 2026). Retailers cannot blindly rely on supplier assurances. Generic terms like "eco-friendly," "green," "climate neutral" are prohibited unless backed by recognised performance standards or third-party certification. Fines up to 10% of global turnover.

### Weather Impact on Retail (March 2026)

2026 has been exceptionally wet. South-west England received 216mm rainfall (184% of long-term average). Named storms Chandra and Goretti brought 50 days of continuous rain. The Met Office issued 154 severe weather warnings in the year to early March.

**Regional impacts:**
- **Somerset:** 29 square miles of Levels and Moors flooded; major incident declared 27 January, not lifted until 18 February
- **Devon/Cornwall:** 373 of 401 flooded properties were in the south-west; Tarka line closed 24 days
- **West Midlands:** Temporary flood barriers deployed along River Severn at Bewdley
- **Thames Valley:** Barriers deployed at Oxford; groundwater flooding in West Sussex
- **Transport:** Parliamentary debate on 19 March on rail infrastructure resilience

**April outlook (Met Office):** Near-average temperatures with classic April showers pattern. High pressure becoming more dominant, particularly in the south, with a northwest-southeast split. Northern and western parts likely to remain unsettled; south and east should see the best of drier interludes.

## 7. PESTEL Analysis: Legal

### CMA Direct Enforcement Powers

Under the DMCCA (in force since April 2025), the CMA can now directly determine consumer law infringements and impose fines of up to 10% of annual global turnover or GBP 300,000 (whichever greater). First penalty imposed 13 February 2026: GBP 473,000 on Euro Car Parks. The CMA has opened investigations into 8 companies and issued advisory letters to 100 businesses across 14 sectors.

Priority areas for 2026-27: AI pricing algorithms, fake reviews (5 new investigations), drip pricing, and greenwashing enforcement.

### Product Safety Overhaul (31 March 2026)

The government launched consultations on major new product safety measures holding online marketplaces to the same standards as physical stores. Consultations close 23 June 2026.

### Data Protection (Data Use and Access Act 2025)

Key provisions in force from 5 February 2026:
- New "Recognised Legitimate Interest" lawful basis for processing
- More flexibility for AI tools in recruitment and fraud detection
- Mandatory complaints process required by June 2026
- PECR penalties aligned with UK GDPR: up to GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global turnover
- EU-UK adequacy decision extended to 27 December 2031

### Retail Crime Legislation

The Crime and Policing Bill has passed the House of Lords:
- Standalone offence for assaulting a shop worker (increased sentencing)
- Removal of the GBP 200 threshold for "low-level" shoplifting
- Introduction of Respect Orders for repeat offenders
- Charges for shoplifting up 21%

### Subscription Contract Rules

New DMCCA requirements (pre-contract disclosure, one-click cancellation, cooling-off periods) pushed back to autumn 2026 at the earliest.

### Other Regulatory Changes

- Energy drinks ban: Prohibited from selling high-caffeine drinks (>150mg caffeine/litre) to under-16s
- Tobacco & Vapes licensing: New regime under the Tobacco and Vapes Bill may come into force with less lead time than retailers expected
- Electronic Commerce Regulations: Repeal of the Country-of-Origin Principle from the Electronic Commerce Directive Regulations

### School Holidays (Easter 2026)

Most schools break up around 30 March - 3 April and return 13-20 April. This two-week window is critical for:
- Family-oriented retail (clothing, toys, outdoor)
- Leisure and tourism spend
- Grocery volumes (meal occasions shift from workplace to home)
- Regional footfall patterns (coastal/tourist areas benefit)

### Ramadan 2026

Ramadan runs approximately 28 February - 30 March 2026, with Eid al-Fitr around 30-31 March. Key for food retail (pre-dawn and iftar meal occasions), modest fashion, and gifting. Areas with significant Muslim populations (Tower Hamlets, Birmingham, Bradford, Leicester) see distinct trading patterns.

### Trading Shape Drivers

1. **Easter weekend (3-6 April):** THE peak trading event -- grocery, gifting, seasonal clothing, garden/outdoor, DIY, travel. Earlier Easter = cooler weather = weaker garden/outdoor vs 2025.
2. **School holidays (w/c 30 March to w/c 13 April):** Family spend shifts; coastal/leisure destinations benefit; commuter-belt footfall weakens.
3. **Grand National (9-11 April):** Merseyside hospitality peak; national betting/entertainment uplift.
4. **April cost wall:** NLW, business rates, employer NI increases all land simultaneously. Retailers likely to offset via price increases, reduced hours, slower recruitment.
5. **Energy price uncertainty:** Middle East conflict trajectory will dominate sentiment. Every week of elevated oil/gas prices compounds the consumer squeeze.
6. **Weather:** If April delivers sunshine, expect a meaningful footfall and seasonal category bounce. If wet conditions persist, expect continued online migration.
